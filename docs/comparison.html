Feature comparison matrix

Where Mini Numbers wins

  • Light and complete. One MIT-licensed program on SQLite with goals and AI referrals built in, and funnels, segments, revenue attribution, signed webhooks, email reports and widgets as labs you switch on. The other light tools (GoatCounter, Liwan, Medama) stop at page views and events.
  • Nothing held back. Plausible CE lacks funnels, revenue, journeys, annotations and the AI channel its cloud has; Rybbit's web vitals are cloud-only; Pirsch self-hosting is enterprise-only. Mini Numbers has one edition.
  • Privacy you can configure. A forget-visitors window from one hour to a year and three modes whose stored fields are checked by tests — and the dashboard tells you which is active.
  • No heavy stack. Plausible needs ClickHouse and 2 GB, Rybbit and Swetrix ClickHouse plus Redis, OpenPanel 4 GB, PostHog 16 GB.

Where the field wins

  • Table stakes from 2025–26. Annotations, MCP access for AI assistants, bot filtering beyond the user agent and journeys are now common; Mini Numbers has none yet.
  • Breadth. Umami 3.x added replay, heatmaps, web vitals and 2FA; Rybbit, OpenPanel and PostHog go further into product analytics.
  • Community and releases. Umami ~40k stars, Plausible ~30k, Rybbit ~13k. Mini Numbers has no tagged release yet.
  • Hosted plans and free SSO. Most competitors sell hosting; Liwan includes OIDC single sign-on for free.

What changed in the market, 2025–26

  • An AI-assistant referrer channel became standard: Matomo 5.5 (Oct 2025), Plausible cloud (Jun 2026), Fathom (Oct 2026). The next step is separating AI crawlers and agents from people (Rybbit 2.9, Matomo, PostHog).
  • MCP servers let AI assistants query analytics: Umami, Rybbit, OpenPanel, PostHog, Seline. Plausible and Fathom only have community-built ones.
  • Self-host stacks got heavier while a quiet lightweight tier improved: Liwan 1.8 (Oct 2026) added events, teams and API keys; Medama runs in 256 MB.
  • Projects went quiet: Vince has had no release since Sep 2025, Shynet ships security fixes only, Ackee only maintenance — their users are looking for a new home.

Not in the race, by choice

  • Session replay, click heatmaps, error tracking. They need far more data about each visitor than a privacy-first promise allows.
  • Feature flags and A/B testing. Product-analytics territory (PostHog, Swetrix, Pirsch).
  • A hosted plan. The pitch is "run it anywhere, even a small VPS", not a subscription.
